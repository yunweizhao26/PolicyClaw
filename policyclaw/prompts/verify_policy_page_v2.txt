How the page is shown: the page was captured from the web (a crawl of websites, or the privacy-policy link of an app-store listing) and converted to plain text, so it may contain menus, footers and banners around the main content. The first lines give the page's URL and its length in words. A page longer than 1,800 words is cut: you see its first 1,500 words and its last 300 words, and a marker line between them gives the number of omitted words. Follow the rubric's instructions for cut pages: when the shown beginning and end belong to one policy, treat the omitted middle as the rest of that policy.

First collect the evidence for the rubric's items, then choose the label. Reply with one JSON object:
- items: for each item below, one sentence copied exactly from the page that states the item, or an empty string when no sentence on the page states it. The sentence must itself state the item: a heading, a link, or a sentence about another topic does not count. On a cut page, leave an item empty when no shown sentence states it, even if the omitted middle may state it.
  - organization: a sentence that names the organization whose policy this is. A placeholder left in a template is not a name.
  - collection: a sentence that states what personal data the organization collects.
  - use_or_sharing: a sentence that states how the organization uses or shares that data.
  - rights_retention_contact: a sentence that states a right or choice of the person, how long the data is kept, or how to contact the organization about privacy.
- reason: one or two sentences that explain the label. For a policy page, say which of the three required items (collection; use or sharing; rights, retention or contact) the page states or lacks.
- label: one of complete_policy, partial_policy, cookie_only, other_legal, not_policy. On a page shown whole, complete_policy requires a sentence for collection, use_or_sharing and rights_retention_contact.
- flags: multiple_orgs (true when the page holds the separate privacy policies of two or more different organizations) and language (the ISO 639-1 code of the main content, or und when the page has no readable text).
