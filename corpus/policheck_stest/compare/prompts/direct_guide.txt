Finding contradictions in a privacy policy

Read the whole policy and list every pair of statements that conflict. A pair conflicts when all three hold:
1. One statement says that a party collects, uses, shares, sells, retains, or otherwise processes some kind of data,
   or may do so. The other says that the same party does not, will not, or may not do it.
2. Both statements cover at least one common real situation: the same kind of data (or one kind includes the other),
   the same kind of processing, the same recipient or source, and conditions that can hold at the same time.
3. The policy gives no way to tell which statement governs in that situation. There is no exception that one
   statement makes for the other, no narrower case carved out of a general statement (for example "except",
   "unless", "children under 13", "sensitive data", "if you opt out"), no statement of precedence, and no
   difference of time, region, product, or user group that separates the two.

The statements may be in different sections and may be far apart.

Do not report:
- a general practice next to a carve-out the policy states, or a prohibition next to its own stated exception;
- statements about different products, services, regions, or user groups;
- a choice the user can make (opt out, delete, withdraw consent) next to the default practice;
- a conflict that exists only under an uncharitable reading of vague wording.

For each conflict, give:
- kind: "contradiction" when, read literally, the two statements cannot both be true in the common situation;
  "unresolved" when the conflict comes from general or vague wording and the policy does not say which governs.
- permit: the exact words of the permitting statement, copied from the policy (one sentence or less).
- prohibit: the exact words of the prohibiting statement.
- situation: a situation in which both statements apply, at most 30 words.
- reason: why the policy does not resolve the conflict, at most 40 words.
- confidence: "high", "medium", or "low".

An empty list is a valid answer. Report only pairs that meet all three conditions.
